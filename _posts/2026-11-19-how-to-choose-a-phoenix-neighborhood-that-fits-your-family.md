Many buyers know they’re ready to purchase but not where in Phoenix Metro they actually want to live. Sorting that out comes down to a few factors, and your price range sits at the top of the list.

**1\. What is your price range?** It’s the main factor deciding which neighborhoods you can realistically be in. I always recommend getting [pre-qualified with a lender](https://blog.samlevyrealestate.com/the-importance-of-getting-pre-qualified-before-buying-a-house.html){: target="_blank" rel="noopener"} first.

Everything else comes back to [what you can afford](https://www.nerdwallet.com/mortgages/how-much-house-can-i-afford/calculate-affordability){: target="_blank" rel="noopener"} and how much you’re willing to spend on a down payment, the full purchase price, and the monthly payment.

**2\. What is your lifestyle?** Some buyers don’t care much about lifestyle and just want [as much home as they can get](https://blog.samlevyrealestate.com/how-to-prioritize-must-haves-vs-wants-when-buying-a-home.html){: target="_blank" rel="noopener"} in square footage and land. If lifestyle matters more to you, start with where you already spend your time.

Think about where [your favorite restaurants](https://blog.samlevyrealestate.com/where-to-hang-out-in-north-central-pheonix.html){: target="_blank" rel="noopener"} and bars are and where your kids go to school. All of these things play into which neighborhood is right for you.

{% include pullquote.html %}

**3\. How’s your commute time?** Many people don’t want to spend 30 or 45 minutes each way getting to and from work. **If you don’t mind the drive, your options open up a lot.**

**4\. Are you single or starting a family?** Generally speaking, you’ll want to live around people who are like-minded. A single buyer might gravitate toward one part of town. A buyer who has a family or is thinking about starting one might want to look somewhere else.

With three kids, you probably don’t want to live in the heart of Old Town Scottsdale. A younger buyer with no kids probably doesn’t want a neighborhood that’s 95% families either.
